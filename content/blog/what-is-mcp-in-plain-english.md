People sometimes ask what the clever part of my setup is. They expect something about prompts, or a special model.

The honest answer is a plug. A boring, standard plug, the same way USB is boring. And like USB, it changes what's possible precisely because it's boring.

## The problem it solves

Until recently, every AI app was an island. If you wanted it to know something about your life, you pasted it in. Each app built up its own memory of you, which you couldn't see, couldn't organise and couldn't take with you.

So you ended up with a little bit of yourself scattered across every chatbot you'd ever used, and none of them had the whole picture.

## The USB idea

Remember when every phone, camera and printer had its own cable? USB fixed that by agreeing on one plug. Any device, any computer.

MCP does the same thing for AI. The letters stand for Model Context Protocol, but you can forget that immediately. It's an agreed-upon plug between AI apps and the places your stuff lives: your notes, your to-do list, your contacts.

The place offers a plug. Any AI app that understands the plug can connect to it.

## What happens when I ask a question

Here's a made-up example from my demo notebook. I ask: "What's left to do for Mum's birthday?"

1. **The AI sees what the plug offers.** Things like "find notes", "list to-dos" and "add a to-do". These are technically called tools; think of them as the buttons on the other side of the plug.
2. **It picks the one it needs**, and asks for just that: the open to-dos under the "Plan Mum's birthday" plan.
3. **It gets back only that.** Three to-dos. Not my whole notebook.
4. **If it's allowed to, it writes back** through the same plug. "Add a to-do: book the restaurant by Friday."

Then it answers me in plain English. My notebook stays where it is. The AI just visited.

## Why it matters

- **Swap the mind, keep the memory.** When a better AI comes out, I connect it to the same plug. Nothing to export, nothing to re-teach.
- **Use more than one.** One AI for writing, another for code, another on my phone. They all read the same memory, so they all give answers based on the same facts.
- **Nothing gets copied wholesale.** The AI asks for what it needs, when it needs it. My life doesn't get uploaded into somebody else's app.

## What it isn't

A few things people assume that aren't true:

- **It isn't an AI.** It's the connection. The thinking still happens in whichever AI app you use.
- **It isn't a place to store things.** Your memory lives wherever you keep it. The plug just lets AIs reach it.
- **It isn't automatically safe.** A plug lets things through. What protects you is the key you use to connect, and what that key is allowed to open.

## Three questions before you plug anything in

1. **Whose server holds the data?** If the place behind the plug belongs to a company, your life is still on their computers, just organised. That might be fine for you. Just know which it is.
2. **What can this key open, and can I take it back?** Good setups give each AI app its own key, opening only what it needs, and let you cancel it in one click. Money is the obvious thing to put behind a key of its own.
3. **Can it write, or only read? And is there a record?** Writing is where the value is, because the AI can file things for you. It's also where mistakes happen. I want a diary line for everything an AI changes.

## Which apps support it

More every month. Most of the big AI apps now have a "connectors" or "integrations" setting, and that's usually this plug underneath. Coding tools picked it up first; chat apps on the desktop and phone have followed.

## The short version

MCP is a standard plug between AI apps and your stuff. It's why your memory doesn't have to live inside any one AI, and why you can change your mind about which AI to use without starting over.

It's boring. That's the point.
