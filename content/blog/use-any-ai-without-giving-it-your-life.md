Two completely different AI apps. Same question. Same answer.

That's because neither of them *owns* my memory. They both just *read* it. And that's the second part of the cyborg: processing.

## Why the AI shouldn't own your memory

Last week we got your life into one organised place. The obvious next step would be to paste it all into your favourite chatbot and let it remember. Here's why I don't.

1. **AI changes fast.** The best AI this year probably won't be the best next year. If your memory lives *inside* one app, switching means starting from zero.
2. **Different AIs are good at different things.** One writes well, one makes great images, one is cheap and quick. Your memory should work with all of them.
3. **It's your life.** Your money, your family, your notes. *You* should decide what an AI can see, and be able to switch it off.

So here's the rule I live by:

> Swap the mind. Keep the memory.

## How it plugs in

So how does an AI read your memory without taking it?

Think of USB. Any USB device works with any computer because they agree on the same plug. There's now a plug like that for AI. It's called MCP, but you don't need to remember the name.

Your organised notebook offers a plug. The AI connects to it, asks for just what it needs ("what are my goals?", "add this to-do"), gets just that, and writes back through the same plug. The AI never gets a copy of your notebook. And when a new AI comes out, it uses the same plug.

## Keys you can take back

To plug in, the AI needs a **key**. Not your password: a separate key, made just for that one AI. My three rules for keys:

- **One key per app.** Every AI gets its own.
- **Only open what's needed.** A key can open the whole notebook or just part of it. My money section sits behind its own key.
- **Take it back anytime.** If I stop using an app, I cancel its key. Nothing else breaks.

AI is the processor, not the vault.

## Connecting it

Connecting takes a few minutes, and it's mostly copy and paste. I use Claude, but plenty of AI apps can do this now. Make a key in your notebook app, paste it into the AI app's connector settings, and you're done.

Then ask it something only *your* memory knows: "What are my goals, and what's planned under each one?" If the answer lists your actual goals, plans and to-dos from last week, it's reading your stuff, not guessing.

## Recipes: teach it once

Plugging in is half of it. The other half is teaching the AI *how* you like things done. That's what recipes are for. Officially they're called "skills"; think of them as saved recipes.

A recipe is a short written instruction that says:

- **When** to use it.
- **Where** things go in your memory.
- **How** you like it done.
- And to **write it in the diary**, so there's a record.

You write it once, in normal language, and the AI follows it every time. No more explaining yourself over and over.

### Recipe 1: "remember this"

The one I use most. In plain English: *if I mention something to do, make it a to-do under the right plan, or ask me which one. If I figured something out, save it as a how-to note and connect it to related notes. Don't make duplicates. Write it in the diary.*

Here's a made-up example from my demo notebook. One rambly message:

> Finally fixed the Wi-Fi dropping: the router was on an auto-update at 3am that kept failing. Turned it off. Also remind me to call the internet company this weekend about the speed.

What comes out:

- A how-to note, "Wi-Fi drops: router auto-update at 3am, turn it off", linked to "How I reset the router".
- A to-do, "Call internet company about speed", due this weekend, under the "Fix home Wi-Fi for good" plan.
- One line in the diary.

That's the fix-I-forgot problem from the first post, solved by one sentence.

### Recipe 2: the weekly review

The seed of next month's learning loop. In plain English: *look at everything I finished, started or got stuck on in the past seven days. Group it by goal. Tell me honestly what moved and what didn't, based only on what actually happened. Suggest three priorities for next week, and ask before changing anything.*

Save it now, but don't run it yet. It needs a week of real life to work with.
