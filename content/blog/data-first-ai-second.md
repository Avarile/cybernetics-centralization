Ask any AI "what should I focus on this week?" and you'll get a beautiful answer. Clear priorities, sensible time blocks, a reminder to take breaks.

It's a great plan. It's just a plan for somebody else.

The AI doesn't know your goals, what you finished last week, or what you've been putting off for a month. So it does what it always does when it's missing something: it fills the gap with something that sounds right.

## A dispenser of hallucinations

There's a line I keep coming back to: *computing power without data is a dispenser of hallucinations.*

"Hallucination" is the technical word for an AI saying something false with total confidence. People usually blame the model for it. But most of the time, when the question is about *your* life, the model isn't broken. It's being asked to describe something it has never seen.

When you ask about your own life, there are three gaps it has to guess its way across:

1. **Who you are.** Your goals, what matters to you this month, what you've already decided against.
2. **What already happened.** What you spent, what you finished, what went wrong last time.
3. **How you do things.** The way you like to plan, the checklist you always use, the shortcut you figured out last year.

None of that is in any AI's training. It can't be. It's yours.

## Why a smarter model doesn't fix it

Every few months a better model comes out, and it's tempting to think *this* one will finally get it. It won't, for this kind of question. A smarter guesser is still guessing. It just guesses more fluently, which makes the wrong answers harder to spot.

The facts about your life have to come from somewhere. Either you type them in every single time, or they live in a place the AI can read.

> AI makes output cheap. Your own record can't be copied.

## What "data first" means in practice

"Data" sounds like spreadsheets and dashboards. Here it just means: write down what's true, in a way an AI can find it. Four habits do most of the work.

1. **Facts before advice.** Record what actually happened: what you spent, what you finished, what broke. Advice built on facts beats advice built on vibes.
2. **Everything has the same shape.** A title, a few lines of context, a date, and whether it's still active. When every entry looks alike, any AI can read all of it without special setup.
3. **Link things.** This to-do belongs to that plan, which serves that goal, and it needed this how-to note. The links are what let an AI follow your reasoning instead of inventing its own.
4. **Ask where it got that.** When an answer sounds confident, ask which note it came from. If it can't point to one, treat it as a guess.

## The difference, side by side

Same AI, same question. The only thing that changes is whether it has your record to read. (These are made-up examples from my demo notebook.)

| You ask | Without your data | With your data |
|---|---|---|
| "Plan my week" | A tidy template for an average person | Your three goals, what's overdue, and the promise you made on Monday |
| "How did I fix the Wi-Fi last time?" | Ten generic router tips | The note you wrote two weeks ago, plus the to-do you never closed |
| "Am I over budget on eating out?" | "I don't have access to your finances" | Month by month, with the one month that went over |
| "What did I tell Alex I'd do?" | Nothing, or worse, a guess | The follow-up from your last catch-up |

The right-hand column isn't smarter. It's just better informed.

## A test you can run today

Pick one question about your own life that you'd genuinely like answered. Ask your AI cold. Then paste in a few lines of your actual notes and ask again.

Notice how much of the first answer was filler. That filler is the gap your own record closes, permanently, instead of one paste at a time.

## Pick the place first, the AI second

Most people choose an AI app and then wonder where to put their life. I think that's backwards. Choose the place your record will live first, somewhere you own and can keep for years. Then plug in whichever AI is best this month.

The models will keep changing. Your record is the part that compounds.
