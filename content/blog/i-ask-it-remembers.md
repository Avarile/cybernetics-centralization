It's half seven. I haven't opened a single app. But I already know my three priorities for today, what I'm behind on, and the one thing I keep putting off.

I didn't write this. My memory did.

Week three of the migration has one rule: **you live on it.** Every plan, every question, every "I'll remember that later" goes through the system, for seven days. No setup this week. Just come with me through a normal day.

(The examples below are from my demo notebook with made-up data. It works the same way on my real one.)

## Morning: the brief

Every weekday morning a short brief builds itself from my goals, plans and to-dos, plus last week's review. Three parts I actually use:

- **Priorities, grouped by goal**, so I see *why* something matters, not just another to-do list.
- **What I'm behind on**: the stuff I'd otherwise quietly ignore.
- **My own promises, coming back to me.** "Last week you said you'd call the internet company. Still not done."

That last line is a loop starting to close.

## Mid-morning: "how did I fix this?"

Quarter past ten, and the Wi-Fi drops again. Old me would have spent an hour searching and redone everything from scratch.

> The Wi-Fi is dropping again. How did I fix this last time?

It finds the note I saved two weeks ago (the router's 3am auto-update) *and* points out that the "call the internet company" to-do is still open. Ten seconds. That's the exact moment from the first post: the fix I couldn't remember.

## Lunch: where did my money go?

A money question I've been avoiding:

> How much did I spend eating out in the last six months, month by month? Am I over budget?

A month-by-month answer and a simple chart, with one month flagged as over. This used to mean a spreadsheet and a whole afternoon. Now it's one question.

And the AI doesn't *keep* my money details. It reads them through a key I control, and that's it.

## Afternoon: before I meet someone

At three I'm meeting a friend (in the demo, "Alex") who's helping me plan Mum's birthday.

> I'm meeting Alex at 3:30 about Mum's birthday. Prep me.

It pulls together what's already decided, what's still open, the gift-ideas note, what Alex offered to do last time, and three questions to settle today. It uses my **meeting template** (what I want out of this, what to ask, what to decide), so I'm never the person going "wait, what did we say last time?"

## Evening: one message, all sorted

End of the day, one message, the way I'd actually say it:

> Great catch-up with Alex. They'll book the restaurant by Friday. I finally called the internet company, and they're sending a new router. Idea for later: a monthly reminder to check my eating-out budget.

What happens:

- A note on Alex, plus a follow-up, "Check Alex booked the restaurant", for Friday.
- "Call internet company" marked done.
- A new to-do, "Set up new router", under the Wi-Fi plan.
- The idea saved for later.
- All of it connected, and all of it in the diary.

Behind every one of these, the same four steps run: **read** the right part of my memory, **apply** my way of doing things, **write** the result back, and **log** it. Captured, guided, tracked.

## What actually changed

Honestly, I'm not doing everything faster. Some things take just as long.

What changed is that **nothing falls through the cracks.** Questions get answered from facts, not guesses. Fixes don't get lost. Promises come back to me. And I'm not carrying all of it in my head anymore.

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| "Where was that note?" | "How did I fix this last time?" |
| An afternoon in a spreadsheet | One money question |
| Scrolling chats before a meeting | A prep sheet from my own records |
| Promises I forgot | Promises that come back in the morning brief |
