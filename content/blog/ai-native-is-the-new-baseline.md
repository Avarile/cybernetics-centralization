I once spent an evening fixing a problem on one of my servers. I read the docs, tried four things, and finally got it working. A year later the same problem came back, and I had no idea what I'd done.

I'd solved it. I'd learned it. And it was just gone.

That's when I realised I don't have a memory problem. I have a *system* problem. So I'm fixing it, in public, over the next four weeks. I'm turning myself into a cyborg.

No implants. Let me explain.

## My life was full of open loops

Here's where I started. Notes in five different apps. Bookmarks I never reopened. Screenshots I never looked at again. I couldn't tell you where my money went last quarter without an afternoon in a spreadsheet. And every January I'd set goals that had gone quiet by March.

Nothing connected, so nothing improved. I'd plan something, do it, and move on. Nothing came back round. Nothing made next time easier.

I call these **open loops**. And if you don't capture something, you can't get better at it.

## Everyone has AI now. So what?

Why am I doing this *now*? Because something big just happened, and most people haven't noticed what it means.

Everyone has AI now. Your colleague, your kid, your competitor. Anyone can get a decent email, plan, report or piece of code in five seconds. **AI makes output cheap.** Being able to produce stuff isn't special anymore.

So what's left? What makes one person's AI more useful than another's?

It's whether the AI knows *your* life: your goals, the things you've figured out, the mistakes you've already made, the way you like to do things. Nobody can download that. You have to build it. **Your own record can't be copied.**

And here's the part that got me: that record compounds. Someone who starts keeping it today gets a little better every week, because every week builds on the last. Someone who doesn't stays where they are. The gap between those two people doesn't stay the same size. It widens, every week.

> The gap compounds. So does the advantage.

So I've stopped thinking of this as a nice extra. Being AI-native is becoming the baseline, and the later you start, the further back you begin.

## The trap: "just use AI more"

Using AI more doesn't close the gap. There are two ways it goes wrong.

**Generic answers.** Ask an AI about your life without your data, and you get advice that sounds great and fits nobody. There's a line I keep coming back to: *computing power without data is a dispenser of hallucinations.* Basically, a very confident guesser.

**Rented memory.** When you *do* tell it about your life, where does that go? Into that one app's memory. You can't really see it, you can't organise it, and if a better AI comes out next year, you can't take it with you. You're renting your own memory.

Using AI that way doesn't make you AI-native. It just makes you look busy.

## What I mean by "cyborg"

You're already a little bit cyborg. Your phone remembers phone numbers so you don't have to. Your calendar remembers your appointments. Your brain is already handing work to systems outside your head.

I just want to do it *on purpose*, properly, with AI. A cyborg, the way I use the word, is **a mind extended by systems you own**. And it has three parts.

### 1. Memory: data first

One organised place for my life: goals, projects, to-dos, the things I've figured out, the people I work with, my money, and a diary of what got done. Not ten apps. One place, and it belongs to *me*, not to an AI company.

Think of it as one very organised notebook that any AI can read.

### 2. Processing: any AI

That's the AI. It reads my memory and helps me use it, but it doesn't *own* it. If a better or cheaper model shows up tomorrow, I plug that one in instead, and my memory stays exactly where it is.

**Swap the mind. Keep the memory.**

### 3. Learning: a closed loop

Once a week, I look back at what *actually* happened, from the record, not from my memory of it. What worked becomes a template I reuse. What went wrong becomes a lesson the system remembers. Mistakes become memory.

This is the part that makes it compound.

**Memory. Processing. Learning.** Or in one line: *My memory. Any mind. Closed loops.*

## What it looks like day to day

I've been building this for a while, so here's what a normal day looks like now. I just ask.

- "How much did I spend on food over the last six months?" Answered from my real records, in seconds.
- "How did I set up that server last year?" It finds the note I wrote back then.
- "Plan my week around my goals." And it actually knows what my goals are.

Behind each question, the same four things happen: the AI reads the right part of my memory, applies my way of doing things, writes the result back, and logs what it did. Nothing gets filed by hand.

## Before and after

| Before | Cyborg |
|---|---|
| Open loops | Closed loops |
| Notes in ten apps | One personal record |
| Reviews skipped | Reviews from facts |
| Know-how forgotten | Know-how compounds |
| Memory rented from an app | Any AI, my data |
| Falling behind quietly | Compounding every week |

## It's simpler than it sounds

If you're thinking "I'm not techy enough for this": you don't need to code, and you don't need to build an app. It's an AI you already know how to talk to, plus one organised place that you own.

The privacy part matters, so I'll say it plainly: my money and my notes stay in my own place, behind my own keys. The AI only gets in through keys I control, and I can take them back at any time.

(For the curious: I use Claude, connected through MCP to a database I host myself. It works with other AIs too, and I'll show the setup later in the series.)

## The 4-week migration

Here's the challenge, and I'd like you to do it with me. One step a week:

1. **Week 1: Memory.** Get your goals, plans and "how I fixed it" notes into one place.
2. **Week 2: Processing.** Connect an AI to it, and save two recipes: one for capturing, one for a weekly review.
3. **Week 3: Live.** Run every day through it for seven days.
4. **Week 4: Loop.** Your first honest weekly review.

I'm not going to promise you'll be some percentage more productive. Nobody can honestly promise that. What I can promise is this: after four weeks you'll have your *own* data to judge it by. That's the point.

## Homework (five minutes)

Write down three things you keep forgetting, redoing or losing track of. The fix you can never remember. The goal that keeps slipping. The thing you always have to look up again.

Keep that list. Next week, it's the first thing that goes into your memory.

Everyone has AI now. The difference is what *your* AI knows. Let's start.
