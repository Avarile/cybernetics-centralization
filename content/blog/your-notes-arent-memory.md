I had years of notes on my phone. When I asked an AI to help me plan my month, it couldn't use a single one of them.

The AI wasn't the problem. My notes weren't *memory*. They were a pile.

This is week one of the migration, and it has one job: turn the pile into a memory.

## A pile vs. a memory

Last week I argued that everyone has AI now, and that what makes the difference is whether your AI knows *your* life. Here's the catch: it can only know your life if your life is **organised**.

Think about your notes app. Every note is just a blob of text. It doesn't know if it's a to-do, a plan, or something you figured out. It doesn't know what it's connected to. It doesn't know if it's still true.

Now think about a really good human assistant. They'd keep things in proper folders. They'd know this task is part of that plan, and that plan is for this goal. They'd remember *how* you solved something last time.

That's what we're building. Not a pile. A memory.

## The four rules of a good memory

I built mine around four simple rules.

1. **One place.** Not one app for notes, one for to-dos and one for money. In real life these things are connected, so they live together.
2. **Everything has a type.** Is it a goal? A plan? A to-do? Something you figured out? A person? An expense? Once things have a type, an AI knows what to do with them.
3. **Connect things.** This to-do is part of this plan. This plan is for this goal. This to-do needed this how-to note. Those links are what let you ask, months later, "why did I do it that way?"
4. **Keep what happened.** Don't delete your history. What you spent, what you finished, what went wrong: those are facts, and facts are what an AI needs to stop guessing.

> One record. Not rented.

## A map of my memory

Here's the shape of my own memory, drawn as a map rather than a screenshot, because the real one holds my real life.

- **Down the middle:** the goals I'm working towards, the plans for each goal, and the to-dos for each plan.
- **On one side:** everything I've figured out: how-tos, fixes, notes. Each one can be linked to the to-dos that used it.
- **On the other side:** the people in my life and work.
- **Money:** what I spend, and what I planned to spend.
- **At the bottom, two things most people never have:** my *templates* (how I like to plan a project, prepare for a meeting or review my week) and a *diary*: one line for everything that got done, whether I did it or the AI did.

Every entry has the same shape: a title, a free-text context, dates, and whether it's still active. That sameness matters more than it sounds. It's why any AI can read all of it without special setup.

You don't need all of this on day one. Start with the middle and the "figured out" side: goals, plans, to-dos and how-to notes.

## What it actually looks like

Honestly? Like a spreadsheet. Lists with columns.

It's technically called a database, but think of it as one very organised notebook whose pages know about each other. Open a goal (in my demo notebook, "Save for a new laptop") and you see its plans. Open a plan and you see its to-dos. Open a to-do and you see which note it needed.

I use one called Teable, which I run myself, but plenty of tools look and work like this. The starter template takes about ten minutes, and from next week the AI does most of the filling-in for you.

## Putting your three things in

Last week's homework was to write down three things you keep forgetting or redoing. Here's how three typical ones land (made-up examples from my demo notebook):

| What you wrote down | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| "I keep re-learning how to reset the router" | A **how-to note**: "How I reset the router", written now while you remember |
| "Getting fit for the hiking trip keeps slipping" | A **goal**, with one **plan** under it and three **to-dos** |
| "I always have to look up which tax things I can claim" | A **how-to note**, linked to the "Sort out tax return" to-do |

That's it. Three things, in one place, organised and connected. Right now it's just a tidy notebook. Next week, we give it a brain.

## Homework for week 1

- Put your three things in, organised and connected.
- Add one real goal, with one plan and three to-dos.
- Write five "how I did it" notes: five things you've had to figure out more than once.
