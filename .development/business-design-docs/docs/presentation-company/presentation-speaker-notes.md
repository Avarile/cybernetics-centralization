# The AI-Native Company: Speaker Notes

About 30–40 seconds per slide. Pause the video during a HOLD if you need more time.

**01: Title**
"Today I want to propose a different way of running our operations: building around our own data, with AI as the operating layer. The broken ring is today. By the end, you'll see why it closes."

**02: Today**
"Look at how we work today. We plan, we execute, and we move on. The information sits in Wrike, email, chat, spreadsheets and people's heads. Ask how much repeated mistakes cost us, or what we lost when someone left, and we can't answer. Not because nobody cares, but because nothing captures it. That's the problem we're solving."

**03: The AI trap**
"The obvious move is to buy AI tools. That brings two risks. First, AI without our data produces answers that look convincing but aren't grounded in our reality. Second, every platform we feed our data into becomes a place we can't easily leave."

**04: Three principles**
"So the approach rests on three principles: data first, AI agnostic, and closed loops."

**05: Data first**
"We keep one database that we own: a record of facts for what has happened, plus an operational layer for what's happening now. Goals, projects, tasks, knowledge, clients and costs are all linked, so AI can work from the real picture."

**06: AI agnostic**
"AI is the worker, not the vault. Our data never lives on an AI platform. We connect whichever model fits the job, and if a better or cheaper one appears, we switch without migrating anything. The same data can produce reports, code, images or campaign material."

**07: How work flows**
"For staff, the day-to-day experience is simple: they ask in plain language. Behind the scenes, a skill pulls the relevant knowledge and history, applies our standard way of working, creates the tasks, and records everything. Three things happen automatically: work is captured, guided by our templates, and tracked."

**08: Closed loop**
"This is where it pays off. Because the full chain of who did what, when and based on which information is recorded, every project can be reviewed in a proper plan–do–check–act cycle. Lessons feed the next project, so the company gets better with every lap instead of repeating mistakes."

**09: Outcomes**
"For leadership, that means planning with full context, weekly reports that write themselves from what actually happened, retrospectives based on the real record, any dashboard on request, and safe sharing."

**10: The shift**
"To sum up the shift: open loops become closed loops, scattered data becomes one source of truth, manual reporting becomes generated reporting, and knowledge stays with the company."

**11: Lean and safe**
"It's also low risk. There's no new platform to build, no codebase to maintain, and no vendor holding our data. It's an AI client plus our database. Each person connects with their own access token and sees only what they're permitted to see."

**12: The ask**
"I can't show you ROI figures today, and that's exactly the point: we don't capture the data that would produce them. The pilot changes that. I'm asking for four weeks with one department: set up the data and access, build the core workflow skills, run it live, then review the results together and decide."
