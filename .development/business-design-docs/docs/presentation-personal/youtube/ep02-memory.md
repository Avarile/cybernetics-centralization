## [0:35] A PILE VS. A MEMORY

**[SIDE]** A split graphic. Left: grey note blobs in a heap. Right: the same information as neat white cards, connected by lines (to-do → plan → goal; to-do → "how I did it"). → **Clip: E2-02**

"Quick recap: last week I said everyone has AI now, and the thing that makes the difference is whether your AI knows *your* life.

But here's the catch: it can only know your life if your life is *organised*.

Think about your notes app. Every note is just a blob of text. It doesn't know if it's a to-do, a plan, or something you figured out. It doesn't know what it's connected to. It doesn't know if it's still true.

Now think about a really good assistant, a human one. They'd keep things in proper folders. They'd know this task is part of that plan, and that plan is for this goal. They'd remember *how* you solved something last time.

That's what we're building. Not a pile. A memory."

## [2:00] THE 4 RULES OF A GOOD MEMORY

**[SIDE]** Four rule cards appear one by one beside me. → **Clip: E2-03 (plus the single-rule highlights); E2-04 at rule three**

"I built mine around four simple rules.

**Rule one: one place.** Not one app for notes, one for to-dos, one for money. One place. Because in real life, these things are connected.

**Rule two: everything has a type.** Is it a goal? A plan? A to-do? Something you figured out? A person? An expense? Once things have a type, an AI knows what to do with them.

**Rule three: connect things.** This to-do is part of this plan. This plan is for this goal. And this to-do needed this how-to note. Those connections are what let you ask, months later, 'why did I do it that way?'

**Rule four: keep what happened.** Don't delete your history. What you spent, what you finished, what went wrong: those are facts, and facts are gold for an AI."

**COMMENT PROMPT:** "Which rule do you break the most? 1, 2, 3 or 4?" → **Clip: G-03**

## [3:40] A MAP OF MY MEMORY

**[SIDE]** A simplified version of [DECK 07]: rounded white boxes with plain-English labels: GOALS → PLANS → TO-DOS down the middle, HOW-TOS & NOTES on the right, PEOPLE on the left, MONEY at the bottom right, and MY TEMPLATES and DIARY at the bottom. → **Clip: E2-05 (plus the dimmed version for "start with the middle")**

"Here's a map of my own memory.

Down the middle, the **goals** I'm working towards, the **plans** for each goal, and the **to-dos** for each plan.

On the right, everything I've **figured out**: how-tos, fixes, notes. Each one can be linked to the to-dos that used it.

On the left, the **people** in my life and work.

Bottom right, **money**: what I spend, and what I planned to spend.

And at the bottom, two things most people never have. **My templates**: how *I* like to plan a project, prepare for a meeting, or review my week. And a **diary**: a simple log of everything that got done, whether by me or by the AI.

You don't need all of this on day one. Start with the middle and the right: goals, plans, to-dos, and things you've figured out."
