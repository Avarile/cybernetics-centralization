## [0:40] MY LIFE WAS FULL OF OPEN LOOPS

**[SIDE]** [DECK 02]: fragments drift away from the figure. → **Clip: E1-01**

"Here's what my life looked like. Notes in five different apps. Screenshots I never looked at again. Bank statements I only opened when something went wrong. And goals, every January, that had quietly disappeared by March.

I call these *open loops*. I'd plan something, do it, and move on. Nothing came back round. Nothing made next time easier.

And if you don't keep track of something, you can't get better at it."

## [1:40] EVERYONE HAS AI NOW. SO WHAT?

**[CAM]**, then **[SIDE]** [DECK 03]: two figures, two lines, the gap widening. → **Clip: E1-02, then E1-03**

"Now, why am I doing this *now*?

Because something big just happened, and most people haven't noticed what it means. Everyone has AI now. Your colleague, your kid, your competitor. Anyone can get a decent email, plan or summary in five seconds.

Which means being able to *produce stuff* isn't special anymore. Everyone has it.

So what's left? What makes one person's AI more useful than another's?

It's whether the AI knows *your* life. Your goals, the things you've figured out, the mistakes you've already made, the way you like to do things. Nobody can download that. You have to build it.

And here's the part that got me. That stuff *adds up*. Someone who starts keeping it today gets a little better every week, because every week builds on the last. Someone who doesn't stays where they are. And the gap between those two people doesn't stay the same size. It grows. Every week.

So I've stopped thinking of this as a nice extra. Being AI-native is becoming the new normal. And the later you start, the further back you begin."

## [3:00] THE TRAP MOST PEOPLE FALL INTO

**[SIDE]** [DECK 04]: the generic answer crumbles; memory gets locked in a box. → **Clip: E1-04, then E1-05**

"But here's the trap. 'Just use ChatGPT more' doesn't fix it.

Ask an AI about your life without telling it anything, and you get advice that sounds great and fits nobody. There's a line I love: computing power without data is a dispenser of hallucinations. Basically, a very confident guesser.

And when you *do* tell it about your life, where does that go? Into that one app's memory. You can't really see it, you can't organise it, and if a better AI comes out next year, you can't take it with you. You're renting your own memory."

## [3:50] WHAT I MEAN BY "CYBORG"

**[CAM]**, then **[SIDE]** [DECK 05]: the figure with three dashed parts. → **Clip: E1-06, then E1-07**

"So here's my idea. And no, I don't mean robot arms.

You're already a tiny bit cyborg. Your phone remembers phone numbers so you don't have to. Your calendar remembers your appointments. Your brain is already outsourcing stuff.

I just want to do it *on purpose*, properly, with AI. And to do that, a cyborg needs three parts."

## [4:40] THE 3 PARTS

**[SIDE]** [DECK 06] → [DECK 09] → [DECK 11]. The migration bar previews 3/3, then resets to 0/3. → **Clip: E1-08 → E1-09 → E1-10**

"**Part one: memory.** One organised place for my life: my goals, my plans, my to-dos, the things I've figured out, the people I know, my money. Not ten apps. One place, and it belongs to *me*, not to an AI company.

**Part two: processing.** That's the AI. It reads my memory and helps me use it, but it doesn't *own* it. If a better AI comes along, I just swap it in, and my memory stays exactly where it is. Swap the brain, keep the memory.

**Part three: learning.** Once a week, I look back at what *actually* happened, not what I *think* happened, and I adjust. What worked, I keep. What went wrong becomes a lesson the system remembers. And *this* is the part that makes it add up over time.

Memory. Processing. Learning. That's the whole plan."

**[SIDE]** [DECK 15]: the complete figure and the 4-week timeline. → **Clip: E1-13**

"So here's the challenge, and I want you to do it *with* me. One video a week, one step a week.
