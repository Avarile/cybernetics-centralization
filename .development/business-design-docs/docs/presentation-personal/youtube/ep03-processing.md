## [0:35] WHY THE AI SHOULDN'T OWN YOUR MEMORY

**[CAM]**, then **[SIDE]** [DECK 09]: AIs plug in and out, and the core never changes. → **Clip: E3-02 for the three reasons, then E3-03**

"Last week we got your life into one organised place. The obvious next step would be to just paste it all into your favourite chatbot and let it remember.

Here's why I don't do that.

**One: AI changes fast.** The best AI this year probably won't be the best next year. If your memory lives *inside* one app, switching means starting from zero.

**Two: different AIs are good at different things.** One writes well, one makes great images, one is cheap and quick. Your memory should work with all of them.

**Three: it's your life.** Your money, your family, your notes. *You* should decide what an AI can see, and be able to switch it off.

So here's the rule I live by: the AI is the brain you rent. The memory is yours. Swap the brain, keep the memory."

## [2:00] HOW IT PLUGS IN (THE USB IDEA)

**[SIDE]** A simple graphic. An outlined AI shape on the left, the solid memory core on the right, and a small plug between them. Light-dots flow both ways. Then a different AI shape plugs into the same plug. → **Clip: E3-04**

"So how does an AI read your memory without taking it?

Think of USB. Any USB device works with any computer because they agree on the same plug. There's now a plug like that for AI. It's called MCP, but you don't need to remember the name.

Your organised notebook offers a plug. The AI connects to it, asks for what it needs ('what are my goals?', 'add this to-do'), gets just that, and writes back through the same plug.

The AI never gets a *copy* of your notebook. And when a new AI comes out, it uses the same plug."

## [3:00] KEYS YOU CAN TAKE BACK

**[SIDE]** [DECK 14]: key icons, each lighting only its own part of the memory. MONEY has its own key. → **Clip: E3-05**

"And to plug in, the AI needs a **key**. Not your password. A separate key, made just for that one AI.

My three rules for keys:
**One key per app.** Every AI gets its own.
**Only open what's needed.** Today I'm recording with a demo notebook full of made-up data, and this key only opens that.
**Take it back anytime.** If I stop using an app, I cancel its key. Done. Nothing else breaks.

[YOU: one casual sentence on how you protect money stuff, e.g. 'My money section has its own separate key.']"

## [4:40] RECIPES: TEACH IT ONCE

**[SIDE]** A simple card titled "RECIPE: Remember this". Lines fade in: WHEN · WHERE · HOW · WRITE IT IN THE DIARY. → **Clip: E3-07**

"Plugging in is half of it. The other half is teaching the AI *how* you like things done, and that's where recipes come in. Officially they're called 'skills', but think of them as recipes.

A recipe is just a short written instruction:
**When** to use it.
**Where** things go in your memory.
**How** you like it done.
And **write it in the diary**, so there's a record.

You write it once, in normal language, and the AI follows it every time. No more explaining yourself over and over."
